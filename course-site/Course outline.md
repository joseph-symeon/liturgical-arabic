## Description

This course in Liturgical Arabic will introduce students to Arabic through the prayers, hymns, and responses of the Orthodox Church. Designed to accommodate both complete beginners and those seeking to build upon existing familiarity, the course develops foundational skills in reading, pronunciation, vocabulary, and comprehension through active engagement with familiar liturgical texts and prayers. Students will learn to recognize and understand commonly recurring words and phrases, read Arabic script with increasing confidence, and participate in selected prayers and hymns in Arabic. 

## Scope 

- This course will teach Arabic as it is used in liturgical services of the Antiochian Orthodox Church. Course material will consist entirely of common Orthodox prayers and liturgical texts. 
- As such, the course focuses on Modern Standard Arabic (fuṣḥā, فصحى; naḥwī, نحوي) rather than a spoken, regional dialect. 
- Instruction will emphasize practice in reading, pronunciation, vocabulary, and grammatical comprehension, with limited writing exercises in support of literacy. Because participation in the Divine Liturgy primarily involves understanding and reciting established texts, the course will not emphasize conversational skills.

## Objectives

- Develop the ability to read Arabic script with increasing confidence and to recite and comprehend common Orthodox prayers in Arabic.
- Develop sufficient familiarity with liturgical Arabic to follow portions of the Divine Liturgy in real time.
- Enable more active and meaningful participation in worship when Arabic is used, strengthening the parish’s connection to the Arabic-speaking tradition of the Patriarchate of Antioch and making that tradition more accessible to those who did not grow up speaking Arabic.
## Methodology 

- A web app developed for this course will provide students with practice reading and reciting alongside recordings of Arabic chant as well as vocabulary and comprehension exercises with spaced repetition.
- Beginning with prayers already familiar to an Antiochian parishioner, lessons are built to expand on each other, introducing new vocabulary and phrases alongside familiar constructions. For example, the phrases repeated across the first three lessons are highlighted below:
	- ==يا ربّ==، ==ارحم==.
	- ==يا ربّ==، يسوع المسيح، ابن ==الله==، ==ارحمني==.
	- قدّوس ==الله==، قدّوس القويّ، قدّوس الذي لا يموت، ==ارحمنا==.
	
	- ==Lord==, ==have mercy==. 
	- ==Lord==, Jesus Christ, son of ==God==, ==have mercy== on me. 
	- Holy ==God==, Holy Mighty, Holy Immortal, ==have mercy== on us. 

- Students will be equipped and encouraged to practice using Arabic in their daily prayers. The curriculum prioritizes introductory prayers common to many services and daily prayers. Regular repetition of familiar prayers will increase confidence in pronunciation, reinforce comprehension, and develop fluency through recitation. 
## Format 

- Weekly in-person meetings will
	- progress through the curriculum with instruction in grammar and usage
	- provide guided reading, recitation, and individual pronunciation feedback
	- introduce seasonal prayers and hymns relevant to the liturgical calendar
	- foster a community of students who encourage one another, learn, and pray together
- Progress will be self-paced: students will be encouraged to advance according to their own ability, prior familiarity, and level of commitment, rather than being held to a uniform pace of mastery. 
- Students will have the opportunity to demonstrate their mastery of both recitation and comprehension to course leaders at weekly meetings in order to earn achievements marking their progress through the curriculum.
